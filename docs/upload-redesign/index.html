Upload redesign · thought exercise · no app code changed

Upload, made readable

The Upload page shows every engine setting to every person, and on a laptop the Share button sits about three screens down. Below are three laptop layouts in ECHO's Paper & Ink language, each with a gallery so media can be extended now and posted later, from any device.

9numbered sections a video post can show today
~20settings before the first render (video, LTX + Bytedance)
≈3laptop screens of scrolling to reach Share, in the recreation below
1screen in each redesign at 1440 × 900, with the priced button in view

Short version

  • Pick B, Gallery first. It is the only design where "extend now, post later" is the page itself, and it removes the POST / BATCH switch.
  • Posting later from another device needs a small server addition. The server already records every upload per user for staff, but no route shows a person their own. The gallery needs that route, plus a place to keep draft text. Decided scope: finished renders only. Described in Gallery and backend; nothing is built.
  • Upload never got the editorial pass. editorial.css has no rule for the studio, its segmented controls or the cost panel. The page inherits the Paper & Ink colours and fonts but keeps the old cockpit structure. That is a large part of why it reads as technical.
  • The wording can ship first. The plain-words table needs no layout change and no backend.

All screens are drawn at 1440 × 810: a 1440 × 900 laptop minus browser chrome. They follow the theme of this page; use "Switch theme" to see carbon. Media is stood in for by colour gradients, and credits, lengths and model names are example values.

Where it stands

Today's page, on a laptop

Recreated from UploadPage.jsx, ExtendDeck.jsx, PostDeck.jsx, StudioStage.jsx and global.css, with the app's real labels. It is not a live screenshot: the backend would not start in this session and the page needs a sign-in, so heights are approximate. Case shown: one tall phone video with cloud outpaint. The dashed line is the bottom of the first screen.

What gets in the way

  1. 1The page states a rule, not a plan. "1:1 REQUIRED" says what is wrong. It does not say what will happen or what it costs.
  2. 2Engine names are the interface. Outpaint, LTX, CFG, LoRA, CAS. Each is a decision most people cannot make and do not need to.
  3. 3Everything has equal weight. Uppercase labels, numbered cards and a badge on every section. Nothing reads as "do this next".
  4. 4The price is two screens down and written for accounting. People need one number, beside the button, on the first screen.
  5. 5Render and post are one sitting. A finished render lives only in this page's memory. Reload, and the page forgets it, though the server still has it.

What works and should stay

  • The square stage that shows where the video sits and which parts get filled. It explains the product better than any text, and it stays visible while the right column scrolls.
  • Automatic choices: square files skip extending, detected 3D files publish as-is, the file name becomes the title.
  • The price before any charge, and the fal.ai disclosure. Both stay visible in every design.
  • Slideshow reorder, cover image, hashtags picked up from the caption.
  • The standalone Image and Video pages, as the full cockpit for people who want every dial.

A

Smallest change

Three steps

The current page, cut into Add → Make it square → Post. The stage stays on the left, as today. The right column shows one decision at a time and never scrolls. Square files, 3D files and slideshows skip step 2 without being asked. The gallery is the "My media" tab.

Good

  • Lowest risk: the stage, the config hooks and the publish call are reused as they are.
  • First-time users cannot get lost.
  • Nothing scrolls on a laptop.

Weak

  • The gallery is a side room. Extending ten clips means walking the steps ten times.
  • BATCH stays behind on the Image and Video pages.
  • About a fifth of a 1440px screen is unused beside the form.

Backend

Flow works today

  • Steps 1–3 use the existing endpoints in the existing order.
  • My media on another device needs the list route in Gallery and backend.
B

Recommended

Gallery first

Four layout variations of this option are in My media, four ways. The page is your media, laid out like the Explore contact sheet. Files become tiles the moment they are added, and each tile says what it needs. A fixed panel on the right acts on the selection: one tile to extend or post, several to extend together. That replaces BATCH. A wide screen is used for what it is good at: seeing many things at once.

Good

  • "Extend now, post later" is the default behaviour, not a feature.
  • POST and BATCH merge: one file or twenty, same screen.
  • Uses the laptop's width: ten tiles and the full post form, with no scrolling.
  • It is Explore's contact sheet, so it already looks like ECHO.

Weak

  • Largest rebuild: new page shell, tile list, per-tile state.
  • The preview is smaller than today's stage. A click on it should open the full stage.
  • A slideshow is several tiles posted as one. The server accepts only un-extended photos in a slideshow.

Backend

Needs backend

  • The page is the list of a person's uploads. The server records them but has no route to show them. Details in Gallery and backend.
  • Extending and posting use the existing endpoints. Publish already accepts any finished render the account owns, including batch renders.
C

Fewest decisions

One-screen composer

No steps. After a file is added, one line states the plan and the price, and the post form is already there. On a laptop the caption can be written while the render runs. "Post" extends, shows the result, then publishes. Drafts sit in a tray in the page opener.

Good

  • Fewest decisions of the three. Familiar from other social apps.
  • Render time becomes writing time.
  • The stage stays large, as today.

Weak

  • One button spends credits and starts a post. The "show me the result first" check has to be on by default.
  • The tray handles a handful of drafts, not fifty. It still needs a gallery page behind "All media".
  • Several files at once has no natural home.

Backend

Flow works today

  • "Post" chains the existing process and publish calls in the browser.
  • Drafts on another device need the list route and stored draft text.

Posting later, from any device

Gallery and backend

Checked in server/jobs.py, server/output.py, server/upload_log.py, server/social/models.py, server/routers/uploads.py and server/routers/video.py. Posting later from another device is a requirement, so the gallery cannot live in the browser. This section says what the server already does and what it lacks. Nothing here is built.

Already there

  • Publish takes a render id. POST /api/uploads/publish accepts any completed job_id the account owns. It does not care when, where or on which page the render was made, so batch renders qualify.
  • Finished renders are kept. With object storage on (production), every completed render, paid or free, is restorable by id for 30 days. Without it (local dev), 6 hours.
  • Every upload already has a per-user record. The staff upload log keeps a row in upload_traces: user, kind, file name, status (staged, processing, processed, failed, published), render id and post id. It also copies the original file to a private bucket for staff review.
  • Status by id. GET /api/{kind}/jobs/{id} and its stream work for any known id.

Missing for another device

  • No route shows a person their own uploads. The record is read only by staff routes (/api/admin/upload-logs). A second device has no way to learn the render ids.
  • That record is a log, not a product table. It is written only when the log bucket is configured, and its writes are allowed to fail silently so they never break a render.
  • Draft text is stored nowhere. Title, caption and place exist only in the page until publish.
  • The working copy of an original is short-lived. A file with no render behind it is usable for 6 hours and is lost on restart. That covers "Needs square" tiles, already-square files, 3D as-is files and slideshow photos. The staff copy is not wired to anything a user can do.
  • No small previews. Videos get a full-length browser proxy, images nothing. A gallery of twenty 4K files needs thumbnails.

Decided scope: finished renders only

The gallery syncs finished renders and their draft text across devices. Files that were added but not yet extended stay on the device where they were added, marked "On this device". That needs one read route and the draft fields, and leaves originals for later. It covers the stated need: extend first, decide to post later, from anywhere.

Verdict

Recommendation

Build B, in three passes

For the layout of the gallery page itself, see four variations of B. The pick there is Shelves, not the contact sheet drawn above.

  1. Words and the editorial pass, on the current layout. Apply the plain-words table, put the price on the button, close sections 02–04 into one "Advanced" group, and give the page the opener, tabs and corners the rest of ECHO has. Frontend only.
  2. A "my uploads" route and draft fields. The decided scope: finished renders only. Backend, small: the per-user record already exists.
  3. The gallery page. Tiles, statuses, the fixed panel, multi-select instead of BATCH. Design A's step 2 is the panel for a "Needs square" tile; design C's plan line is its first row.

Why not A or C alone

  • A fixes readability but keeps the gallery as a side room, leaves BATCH behind and wastes laptop width.
  • C is the easiest to read, but still needs a gallery page behind its tray.
  • B costs the most. It is also the only one whose structure matches the request and the screen most people use.

Open questions

  • Do the Image and Video pages stay as the pro cockpit, or does Upload replace them?
  • Is a 30-day limit on unposted renders acceptable to show users?
